## 2. The World Bank brief and what judges reward

Primary source: "04 World Bank x Hack-Nation - Small AI for Development.pdf" (20 pp, Oct 2 2026) in the shared Drive folder ([file](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1gBITLc19bJW7zCqZlSMdcrfbeVsRSPHg/view)); local copy `docs/WorldBank_Small_AI_for_Development_brief.txt`.

**Framing question** (p4, also a required video section): "what does localizing AI development mean for you?" Entries that acknowledge trade-offs are encouraged.

**Persona.** Noor, 38, 2-ha farmer in the fictional Ondera highlands; her own phone (calls, messages, mobile money), daughter's smartphone on weekends, no Wi-Fi, 3G bundles, phone left at the house most of the day. "Noor is fictional; her constraints are not. They are drawn from World Bank-supported work with governments, including primary health care access in Solomon Islands." (The Solomon Islands hyperlink in the PDF resolves only to worldbank.org root.) The brief says "her own phone", never literally "basic phone".

**Health challenge (Annex A, verbatim).** "Design and demonstrate a Small AI solution that improves one meaningful part of Noor's access to primary care or a frontline worker's ability to serve her; for example screening support, documentation, referral, follow-up or continuity of care." Pain points named: overcrowded clinic, doctors "not always up to date with the latest medical guidance", "burdensome record-keeping".

**Hard rules (p7).** Runs on a device the user already has; **core feature works offline**; model files small enough to side-load or send over a weak connection; **at least one interaction in a named local language by voice or text**, and "expect to be asked how the tool would fare in a less-supported one"; human-in-the-loop ("A person makes the final call... it does not act on the user's behalf"); avoid hallucinations. Health annex: "this sector deliberately lists no medical imaging or diagnosis datasets, because interpreting them is out of bounds"; "state where the data sits, who can read it, and what happens when the phone is lost or shared"; "A confident wrong output where a life is at stake is worse than no output."

**Rubric (pp10–11).** Built solution / Small AI fidelity 25%; development relevance and impact 20%; data grounding 15% ("is the data modeling sound?"); evidence it works 15%; clarity, design, inclusivity, value proposition for AI 15% ("would a simpler tool (SMS, a spreadsheet, a search) do the same job?"); scalability, replicability, what happens next 10%; Responsible AI, data and safety pass/fail, requiring a fail-safe such as "not sure — ask a person".

**Data rules (section 07).** Cite every source; "Name every dataset, its source, its license and its size. You must also indicate what your data does not cover, and this is scored"; synthetic data allowed if labelled. Problem evidence must carry source, year, country.

**Video content WB wants** (fold into the three 60-second cuts): the one-sentence template "Because of this tool, [user] will [action] by [when] that they would otherwise [not do / do late / do worse]; we know because [evidence]"; the AI capability and why SMS/spreadsheet/search would not do, plus guardrails; end-to-end demo; where the tool sits in the user's day plus tech stack; "Your take".

**What judges likely reward, read from the weights.** 40% is "does it actually work offline within the constraints" plus "evidence"; 15% is a data card with honest gaps; the glossary uses "fixed list of answers", "store-and-forward", "quantization", so speak that vocabulary. The Small AI definition stresses "a single, well-defined problem"; a general node builder reads as unfocused unless you present it as the engine under one concrete health use case. DHIS2 is named as "the system your record most plausibly lands in", so a DHIS2-shaped store-and-forward record is the cheapest way to score institutional fit.

## 3. Solomon Islands context and design implications

**Population and language.** ~844K people, 73% rural, median age 20.7 ([DataReportal 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-solomon-islands)). 70+ languages in the archipelago (72 indigenous in the Wikipedia table, which includes Bougainville); English official, Pijin the lingua franca ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Solomon_Islands_archipelago)). Pijin speakers: 24,390 L1 / 307,000 L2 in 1999 data. **Refuted:** the claim that Wikipedia reports preliminary 2019 census figures making Pijin the largest L1; neither article says so. Pijin has no government-standardised orthography; standardisation came from SITAG, Jourdan's 2002 trilingual dictionary (Pacific Linguistics 526), Beimers' 2009 grammar (PhD thesis) and 2010 spelling list, and the Bible ([Pijin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pijin)). Expect spelling variance (fas/first, blong/bilong) in input and training data.

**Literacy.** 2019 census (pop 5+, N=631,061): 72.9% can read/write a simple sentence in English, 68.4% in "Pidgin", 64.4% in a local language; Malaita 61.0/55.8; Honiara 87.4/81.0; literacy 15+ 85.5% (rural 82.3%) ([Census Vol 2](https://solomons.gov.sb/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Solomon-Islands-2019-Census-Report-Vol-2_Basic-Tables_Operations.pdf), tables P9.7/P9.8). Functional literacy is far lower: ASPBAE 2011 found 17% literate, 42.7% semi-literate, 40.2% non-literate in Honiara and Malaita vs 76.6% declared; World Bank "Skills for Solomon Islands" (2012) 7–33.4% functional across four provinces ([Devpolicy](https://devpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/wp-post-to-pdf-enhanced-cache/1/alarming-literacy-gap-in-the-pacific-20131122.pdf)). **Implication:** voice-first for patients, short plain Pijin outputs, text is fine for nurses.

**Health system.** Five tiers: nurse aide posts, rural health clinics, area health centres, provincial hospitals, National Referral Hospital; 2010 counts: 7 provincial + 4 private hospitals, 37 AHCs, 100+ RHCs; workforce 2,728 incl. 153 doctors/dentists, 936 nurses, 524 nurse aides ([RRH 2096](https://www.rrh.org.au/journal/article/2096)). WHO 2017: 202 nurse aide posts to close or upgrade, 34 AHCs upgraded with doctors (~100 Cuban-trained graduates), 157 doctors with 126 in the capital, ~30% of births at NRH vs Honiara 13% of population ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/feature-stories/detail/health-closer-to-home-transforming-care-in-the-solomon-islands)). **Refuted framing:** the official MHMS Role Delineation Policy PDF (2018) defines **six** service levels, not four, and contains no "flagship RDP 2018" wording ([MHMS RDP](https://solomons.gov.sb/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/MHMS-Role-Delineation-Policy.pdf)). No formal MHMS CHW cadre exists; NGO volunteers (World Vision, JICA Healthy Village, GAVI village health committees) fill the gap (medium confidence). Referral transport is ferry, occasionally plane or helicopter: triage should separate "refer now" from "refer at next boat" and say what to do while waiting.

**Guidelines (RAG corpus).** The free offline "Solomon Islands Guideline Host" app (Therapeutic Guidelines Ltd + MHMS) carries the Adult STG, Children STG, Antibiotic Guidelines 2015 and O&G STM, English only ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/solomon-islands-guideline-host/id1354860792)). STM for Children 4th ed 2017 (124 pp, UNICEF-hosted, no licence statement; already in `data/raw/`) ([PDF](https://www.unicef.org/pacificislands/media/851/file/Standard-Treatment-Manual.pdf)); O&G STM 3rd ed 2024, 271 pp, explicitly CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([AVI PDF](https://www.avi.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/SI-OG-STM-E-version_final-LR-002.pdf)); NCD primary-care guidelines (28 pp, WHO PEN) ([WHO extranet](https://extranet.who.int/ncdccs/Data/SLB_D1_Solomon%20Is%20booklet%20for%20the%20management%20of%20NCDs.pdf)); TB Guidelines 2024 ([gov](https://solomons.gov.sb/?p=63443)). The adult STM 2011 is not online.

**Burden.** Malaria: 123,476 confirmed cases 2023, Pv 57%, first line AL + primaquine, G6PD test required before primaquine (2023 policy), RDTs free, test positivity ~45% ([WHO profile](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/country-profiles/malaria/malaria-2024-slb.pdf)); API ~185/1,000 in 2024, highest in Asia-Pacific, with RDT/ACT stockouts ([APLMA](https://www.aplma.org/blog/navigating-the-malaria-crisis-in-the-solomon-islands-challenges-and-strategies)). TB incidence 123/100K (2024, WB). NCDs 66.8% of deaths (2019). MMR 122 (2020). Dengue endemic (2016–17: 12,329 suspected cases). **Implication:** encode RDT-first fever logic with "no RDT / no ACT available" branches; dengue warning signs; child danger signs; all from the STM, never a diagnosis.

**Connectivity, power, devices.** 567K cellular connections (67%), 85.9% 3G/4G-capable, but only 358K internet users (42.5%); ISOC: 29% used the internet in 3 months (2024), 40% 4G access, mobile broadband basket 11.61% of GNI per capita ([ISOC Pulse](https://pulse.internetsociety.org/en/reports/SB)). 44.7% of people 12+ own a phone (Honiara 70.1%, Malaita 34.6%, Central 30.6%); only 4.8% of mobile-internet users used it for health (census). Grid electricity in 15.3% of households (3.5% rural); 81.1% light with solar (census H17). Our Telekom covers all 9 provinces 2G/3G/4G; "Redhot Giga" bundles from SBD 6 = 1 GB/1 day, SBD 10 = 4 GB/1 day ([In-depth Solomons](https://indepthsolomons.com.sb/our-telekom-announces-new-redhot-giga-plans-delivering-on-promise-of-better-value-for-customers/)); Starlink class-licensed Aug 30 2024. Android ~85% (Samsung 38%, Xiaomi 16%), Android 11–13 still ~33% ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/solomon-islands)). Facebook/Messenger dominate (Messenger 79K–254K depending on method); **no WhatsApp statistics exist for Solomon Islands** (medium confidence). **Implications:** a sub-1 GB one-time pack that fits in one SBD 10 bundle; low-power inference on solar-charged phones; Android-first, minSdk ~30; make the chat adapter channel-agnostic (Messenger/SMS/WhatsApp) and keep WhatsApp as a documented path, not the core.

**Pijin NLP assets.** Meta MMS covers `pis` for ASR (mms-1b-all adapter) and TTS (mms-tts-pis, 36.3M VITS), both CC-BY-NC-4.0 ([MMS coverage](https://dl.fbaipublicfiles.com/mms/misc/language_coverage_mms.html), [mms-tts-pis](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-tts-pis)). Omnilingual ASR (Apache 2.0) lists pis_Latn with 25.2 training hours ([repo](https://github.com/facebookresearch/omnilingual-asr)). Text: MADLAD-400 `pis` ~1.1K docs, Bible-flagged; NLLB-200 and Google Translate have Tok Pisin only; Qwen3's 119-language list includes Tok Pisin ([Qwen3 blog](https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen3/)). The UniMelb WMT 2026 paper gives the recipe: 42.5K pairs, loanword respelling +7.7 chrF++, joint training with Tok Pisin/Bislama +1.4, Gemma-4-31B zero-shot pis→eng 62.3 chrF++, and a warning that LoRA on small Gemma underperformed NLLB fine-tunes ([arXiv 2609.13615](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13615)). Health NZ publishes a Pijin measles toolkit with clinician Q&A videos ([HealthEd](https://healthed.govt.nz/products/nip9219-pacific-measles-community-toolkit-solomon-aelan-pijin-solomon-islands-pijin)): a licensed few-shot and eval set. Policy hooks: Digital Health Strategy 2025–2029 ([WHO](https://www.who.int/fiji/news/detail/20-01-2025-solomon-islands-ministry-of-health-and-medical-services--paves-the-way-for--digital-health-transformation)), DHIS2 and mSupply/Tupaia in use, WB ECHO project P510479 ($20M IDA, digital family health cards, CHW programme) ([PID](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099062425214519941/pdf/P510479-fa25f5f2-44eb-4e5f-9cf7-9cac6bc7c0e0.pdf)).

**Answer to "a less-supported language":** Pijin, Tok Pisin and Bislama are closely related English-lexifier creoles with 10M+ speakers combined; the same pipeline swaps the MMS/Omnilingual adapter and pools creole data. For the 70 vernaculars with no data: fall back to Pijin voice, and say so.

**Runtimes.** llama.cpp/GGUF everywhere (wllama in the browser PWA, PocketPal or llama-server on Android/laptop, MLX on your Mac); LiteRT-LM only if you ship Gemma 4 E2B (MediaPipe LLM Inference is in maintenance mode); sherpa-onnx for STT/TTS/VAD on phones (Whisper, Moonshine, Parakeet, Piper, Kokoro, Supertonic, Pocket TTS, KittenTTS). sherpa-onnx's documented VITS list does not include MMS, so mms-tts-pis runs via transformers.js (VITS is supported there) or Python.

**Deliberately excluded.** LFM2.5-1.2B (fastest 1B on phone CPU, 719 MB, but the LFM Open License caps commercial use at $10M revenue); MedGemma 1.5 4B (LiteRT-LM int4 2.41 GiB, but gated HAI-DEF terms, English-centric; use only as a baseline number); Apollo2 (Gemma-2 terms, 2024 base); Llama 3.2 (community licence); Qwen2.5-Omni-3B (non-commercial, 18 GB VRAM); Orpheus small tiers (never released); Voxtral TTS (CC-BY-NC, 16 GB VRAM). No Phi-5, no small Llama 4, no SmolLM4, no Qwen3.5-Omni "Light" open weights exist. **Correction:** Qwen3.6 open weights (27B dense, 35B-A3B) shipped in **April** 2026, not May; no sub-4B Qwen3.6 exists, so Qwen3.5 is the small line.

**Fallback chain to implement.** Device RAM below threshold → next-smaller tier; TTS fails to load → text only; Pijin STT unavailable on phone → Pijin text + English voice; retrieval returns no STM support or confidence low → "Mi no sua, askem nes/dokta" and log; red-flag match → replace generation with the STM referral step.
